# Interpreting Indian Institutional Flows

## FII/DII flows

| FII net | DII net | Reading |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Positive | Positive | Broad buying. Supports a rally. |
| Negative | Positive | DII absorbs foreign selling. The market often holds but upside is capped. |
| Positive | Negative | Foreigners lead. DII books profits. Often early in a rally. |
| Negative | Negative | Broad selling. Raise caution. |

- Look at 5 and 20 session totals, not one day. One large day can be index rebalancing or a block trade.
- A net flow under Rs 2,000 crore over 5 sessions is treated as small.
- FII selling with a rising Nifty means domestic money is carrying the market. Check breadth.
- Sustained FII buying with weak breadth means a few large caps lead. Check sector rotation.

## Bulk and block deals

- Bulk deals: a trade above 0.5% of a company's shares in one day. Block deals: large negotiated trades in a special window.
- Net buying by mutual funds or insurers is a stronger sign than net buying by a trading firm.
- Same-day buy and sell by one client is churn, not conviction.
- Large sells by a promoter or early investor are a risk flag. Check the announcement.
- Small caps show the largest effect. A deal worth under 0.1% of market value says little.

## How to combine

1. Market view: flows regime plus market-breadth-analyzer and market-top-detector.
2. Stock view: deals for the symbol, then price trend and volume.
3. Confirm with NSE corporate announcements and shareholding pattern.

## Cautions

- Data is delayed to the end of the session.
- Flows are cash market only. Futures and options flows can offset them.
- Never act on one signal. Orders go only through trader-risk and trader-broker.
